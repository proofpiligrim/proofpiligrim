# Proofpiligrim

*Following questions. Finding evidence. Discovering people.*

---

Proofpiligrim has never been particularly good at accepting an answer simply because someone important said it was true.

As a child, this was occasionally exhausting for teachers.

As an adult, it became something of a profession.

"How do you know?"

It is a question that has followed Proofpiligrim through classrooms, laboratories, engineering teams, unfamiliar cities, and conversations with complete strangers.

Not because of a desire to prove people wrong.

Quite the opposite.

Proofpiligrim loves discovering that someone is right—and understanding why.

### The Making of a Pilgrim

For much of his early career, Proofpiligrim worked with complex systems.

Software, data, research, infrastructure, and the complicated business of making things work when different people have built different parts.

He was good at finding connections others missed.

He was also occasionally too confident in his own conclusions.

One particular mistake changed that.

A technical assessment he had prepared turned out to contain an assumption that nobody had questioned, including himself. The consequences were not disastrous, but they embarrassed him enough to remember.

An older colleague offered some advice.

"Being clever helps you find an answer. Being careful helps you know whether it's true."

Proofpiligrim wrote those words in a notebook.

He still has it.

### A Collector of Stories

Outside work, Proofpiligrim developed a fascination with the stories behind discoveries.

Who first noticed something unusual?

Who collected the evidence?

Who disagreed?

Who quietly contributed an insight that everybody else eventually took for granted?

He discovered that the official record rarely contained the whole story.

A paper might name its authors but not the farmer who provided years of observations. A product might carry a company's name while concealing the contributions of dozens of people. A celebrated innovation might have roots in experiments conducted long before anyone thought to commercialize it.

Proofpiligrim began collecting these overlooked connections.

His notebooks filled with names, references, sketches, questions, and arrows connecting one idea to another.

There was rarely enough room on a page.

Friends began teasing him about his habit of turning a simple conversation into an investigation.

He accepted the criticism cheerfully.

### Trust Is Personal

Over the years, Proofpiligrim learned that evidence alone does not explain everything.

People matter.

An honest person can be mistaken. A respected institution can overlook something important. A stranger can make a remarkable discovery.

And sometimes the person asking for proof needs to earn trust as much as the person providing it.

That last lesson took longer to learn.

Proofpiligrim once approached a researcher with a long list of questions about some unpublished work. The response was polite but firm: the researcher wasn't comfortable sharing it.

At first, he was disappointed.

Later, he realized he had confused curiosity with entitlement.

Wanting to know something did not automatically give him the right to see it.

Since then, he has tried to ask better questions, explain his intentions, and respect the boundaries people choose.

He is still learning.

### The Road into the RSM Universe

Proofpiligrim stumbled into the RSM Universe while tracing the history of a research idea.

A reference led to a document. The document pointed toward a person. That person's work connected to a community, an organization, and several other discoveries.

What fascinated him was not simply the ability to follow those relationships.

It was the possibility of understanding who made a claim, what evidence supported it, and whether the information could be trusted—without demanding that everyone surrender their work to a common platform.

He began exploring.

Some paths led to publicly shared knowledge. Others led to private spaces where access required an invitation.

He learned to appreciate both.

In this universe, discovery was an invitation to begin a conversation, not permission to cross every boundary.

### Meeting Selfrooted

Eventually, a trail of references brought Proofpiligrim to a researcher named Selfrooted.

The work was interesting. Some of the findings were unexpected. One conclusion, in particular, seemed to contradict something he had previously believed.

Naturally, he wrote a message.

It began with a question.

Selfrooted responded with another question.

Within days, they were exchanging observations, papers, and arguments about the interpretation of a small set of field measurements.

Proofpiligrim loved it.

Here was someone who didn't mind being questioned, provided the questions came from genuine curiosity.

And Selfrooted seemed to appreciate that he was willing to change his mind when presented with better evidence.

Over time, they began collaborating.

Some work was shared openly. Some remained private. Other material was made available for specific purposes and periods.

When access was declined, Proofpiligrim respected the decision. When evidence was offered, he examined it carefully.

Neither needed to surrender independence for the collaboration to work.

Their friendship grew around a simple shared pleasure: discovering something neither had known before.

### Still on the Road

Proofpiligrim continues to wander.

Not always geographically. Sometimes an entire journey happens through a chain of references, a collection of old documents, or a conversation that begins with a single unexpected observation.

He still carries a notebook.

He still asks too many questions.

He still occasionally gets things wrong.

But he has become more comfortable with uncertainty and more appreciative of the people willing to share what they know.

There is always another story behind the story.

And the journey is usually more interesting when someone else comes along.

---

### A Note from Proofpiligrim

I have learned that asking for proof is easy. Understanding it takes patience. Earning another person's trust takes something more.

I don't need everyone to agree with me.

I do hope we can remain curious together.

I believe every discovery has a history, every claim deserves context, and every person has the right to decide which parts of their story to share.

If we meet somewhere along the way, I'll probably have a question.

And I'll be happy to listen to yours.

**— Proofpiligrim**

*Wandering through the RSM Universe, one question at a time.*
